## Keep Nova-3 for the fast lane tonight

- [Nova-3 language prompting](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/nova-3-language-prompting)
  documents 55 languages including Japanese, repeated EN/JA hints, batch and
  streaming on v1. Hints bias detection; they do not exclude other languages.
- [Code-switching guide](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/multilingual-code-switching)
  recommends 100 ms endpointing. This is a silence/transport boundary, not proof
  of a sentence ending. Raising it blindly could trade responsiveness for fewer
  chunks without repairing punctuation. Keep the fast lane and contextualize its
  immutable finals separately.
- [Keyterm prompting](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/keyterm) supports Nova-3
  multilingual and Flux, 500 tokens total, focused plain-term lists, usage
  redaction, and mid-stream Configure updates. We retain next-request snapshots
  rather than introducing mid-stream mutation. Contextual spelling confirmations
  are hints, never global substitutions or recognition guarantees.

## Existing intelligence, bounded refinement

Reuse the existing Mind institutional context and bilingual/clarification role,
now with a shared source-grounding contract. Preserve raw speaker IDs rather than
the previous prompt's speculative identity merging. The handoff proposed an
institutional Claude gateway, not an upstream runtime patch. Its dedicated endpoint,
model and secret binding remain unresolved, so no production refinement model is
selected or activated. Raw ASR does not await it.

One small synthetic text replay found Haiku 4.5 repaired a sentence boundary but
left a confirmed fictional colleague's misrecognition and introduced a budget
interpretation in Japanese. Sonnet 5 proposed the contextual name correction,
preserved the payment-brand counterexample and negation, and gave linked bilingual
notes; one validated call took **24.122 s**. This is one observed text-model result,
not a statistical quality/latency comparison and emphatically not WER. Earlier
Sonnet attempts exposed a validator bug rejecting a legitimate empty name reading;
the schema now permits unknown readings instead of encouraging inventions.

Automatic processing uses approximately 30 seconds of new finals or 1,500
characters, bounded by 32 source chunks / 12,000 characters per call. These are
starting bounds, not a scientifically established optimal window. Request-owned
processing, single-flight, timeout, visible failure and human review are more
important tonight than speculative provider migration. All changes, including
punctuation, remain drafts until accepted; lexical edits are separately flagged.

## Audio validation still required

Use a consented, manually adjudicated bilingual audio set with person-name,
payment-card and travel-visa counterexamples; negation, uncertainty, numbers,
dates, accents, noise, overlaps, code-switches and long sessions. Compare the
same original audio and hint inventory across providers. Report WER for English,
CER/tokenization policy for Japanese, entity and negation errors, sentence-boundary
precision/recall, speaker attribution, truncation, first-partial/final/refinement
p50/p95 latency, hallucinated notes and correction regressions. Separate raw ASR
from refined text. Retain audio and reference provenance privately. Text replay,
public demos and green mocks cannot answer these questions.
